## 0. Verdict in one paragraph

**GO-WITH-CHANGES** (not a clean go). The core observation is real and I reproduced it to the digit; the 3' extension itself is mechanically correct
(my independent code agrees with the survey on 4,270/4,270 rows). But five things in the note are wrong or unsupported and must change before any code is written:
(1) the chooser as specified is not an "extension" feature, it silently makes miniprot canonical for about half of all loci and moves the 5' end (which section 3.5 says it will not do);

- The note's "~1-3% for >= 100, well under 0.1% for >= 200" is **correct as iid arithmetic** at the actual GC (0.4-1.4% / <= 2e-4). It is the wrong null.
- Real sequence is not iid. A better empirical null: the open run after a **real** stop (frames +1/+2, and +0, of the downstream region of models whose next codon is a stop; `nullpost.py`, 626 complete models):
  P(open >= 30) = 50-76%, P(>= 100) = 1.4% (frame +0), **16%** (frame +1), 5% (frame +2); P(>= 200) = 0.2-0.5%. Clade extremes: Alternaria frame +1 P(>= 100) = **78%**, Pyrenophora frame +1 19%.
  (Caveat: these include 3' UTR and the next gene on the same strand, and clonal clades are correlated; it is an upper bound, not a calibrated FP rate.)
  Same conclusion from the off-frames of the extension region itself (`myext2.py`): +1/+2 shifted frames from the model end are open >= 100 codons in 20%/4% of all models, in Aureobasidium 22%/32%.
- Consequence: **">= 100 aa accepted on the open frame alone" is not supported** (an unrelated frame in the same region is that long about 5-20% of the time in these genomes, 30%+ in Aureobasidium). >= 200 aa is defensible as an ORF-only criterion (<= 0.5% in all but one clade).
  30-99 aa has essentially no ORF-only evidence (P ~ 50%), so the note's rule of requiring an independent model within 10 aa is the right call - but see 3 for how weak that "independent" model is.

### The null test (design 3.3, `nulltest.py`) [V]
- Statistic: MAFFT `--localpair` identical columns minus 0.5 x overhang, real tail vs shuffled amino acids, `max` over 26 references for the real tail but fixed reference for shuffles. MAFFT identical columns of a ~200-aa tail against a ~360 aa reference is inflated by alignment flexibility (shuffles reach 56-60 vs real 63), so a weak result says as much about the statistic as about the tail.
- A shuffle of amino acids is an adequate null for "no similarity", but not for "tail is a real ORF with the clade's codon bias that is simply distant" (that is the hypothesis one cares about).
- I redid it properly (`nulltail.py`, 2,001 extensions >= 30 aa, Smith-Waterman BLOSUM62 open -11/extend -1, best of all curated references of the gene; nulls: 20 aa shuffles, 10 **codon shuffles** (keeps codon usage), the +1 and +2 off-frame translations of the same region, and the reversed tail):
  real tail beats **all** nulls in only 37% of extensions; beats them by >= 20 raw score in 26%. By clade: Macrophomina (489) 4% (0% by >= 20), Botryosphaeria (142) 0%, Venturia (66) 0%, Phyllosticta 22%, Cladosporium 43%, Aureobasidium 23%, **Pyrenophora (78) 94% (17% by >= 20)**, Hortaea 61%, Didymella 100%, Alternaria 80%.

That is the correct reading of the note's own "5 of 9 runs", but it is worse than the note admits: it is not "marginal", it is absent for the bulk of the data.
  This does not show the tails are wrong (C-termini of MAT1-1-1 are poorly conserved and the references are far), it shows the score gate the note considered and rejected could never have been used.

## 3. Biology and algorithm critique

**"Longer clean, 3'-closed, better scoring" - is it a sound principle?** Partly. "Clean" (no stop, cds_blocks translation) and "3'-closed" are sound hard filters. "Better scoring" is not a principle here, because (next section) the score cannot see the thing being decided. And the principle is silent on the 5' end, which the data say is the larger error class (below).

Failure modes, with what I could measure:

1. **Extension reads through an intron that lies after the alignment edge [R, partly V].** Real gene continues via a short 3' intron; the in-frame run through the intron needs no stop in ~50-100 bp of AT-rich sequence (~15-25%) and then lands in the next exon in a random frame (2/3 chance of a different frame).
   Expected false-extension rate is the product of those, i.e. low single digit percent of genes with a C-terminal intron - not measurable without truth. A cheap proxy to compute before building: the dinucleotide GT directly after the model end (donor site) among extended models - see item 9 below.
2. **Extension into a neighbouring gene (same strand) [V].** Against the *annotated* genes in the same locus (core + flank models in the report): 24 of 2,010 extensions (1.2%) overlap another reported gene; 21 on the same strand
   (MAT1-1-1 into MAT1-2-1 in Phoma, Didymella (x5), Elsinoe, Cladosporium, Bimuria, Ophiobolus, Westerdykella, Sphaerulina, Setomelanomma ...; MAT1-2-1 into MAT1-1-1 in Stagonosporopsis, Macroventuria, Decorospora, Massariosphaeria, Sphaerellopsis).
   These are exactly the tight homothallic-type loci the patch cares about. Unannotated neighbours (non-MAT genes) are not in the reports, so this is a **lower bound** [X for the rest]. The note has no neighbour guard; add one (stop extension at the neighbouring modelled CDS, flag `ext_overlaps_neighbour`).
3. **Over-long extensions [V].** 52 (5.8%) extended MAT1-2-1 models exceed the curated 90th percentile length; MAT1-2-1 extensions of 192-219 aa produced total HMG proteins of 391-454 aa in the three homothallic loci below. A length plausibility guard (e.g. total <= 1.25 x the longest curated record, per gene) is cheap and would have caught them.
4. **Real upstream stop because of a missed intron *inside* the alignment [V].** The note treats these as `interrupted` (good). Quantity: 492 models, but 188 are frameshift models (above); with `cds_blocks` 87 of the 190 I could rebuild become clean, leaving ~405 interrupted (-18%), of which ~304 are non-frameshift. The `after_stop_ref_cols` idea is the right evidence; keep it.
5. **Genetic code [R, partly V].** Only codes 1 and 12 appear. Code 12 (CUG-Ser) changes no stop; "readthrough" and mitochondrial-type stop changes (TGA=Trp, TAA/TAG=Q etc.) cannot occur in nuclear MAT genes of these data; the risk is only a wrong `genetic_code` in a report (6 reports have `genetic_code: null`). Test only with unit tests.
6. **Overlapping / antisense ORFs, polycistronic [R].** Fungal nuclear genes are monocistronic; the MAT1-1-2/1-1-3 genes overlap or nest with MAT1-1-1 in some lineages (Sordariomycetes, Sclerotiniaceae), which is the same-strand overlap in point 2. Not otherwise quantifiable here.
7. **5' end [V] - the largest omission.** 79% of canonical models lack M; in 76% of those an in-frame M exists upstream before the upstream stop (so the model start is *downstream of a possible start*); the `n_term_truncated` rule (no M, in-frame stop within `NTERM_STOP_WINDOW` upstream with no M between) flags 4.9% / 6.1% / 6.3% / 15.7% / 17.1% of models for windows of 10/20/30/60/100 aa (`nt.py`): the flag is extremely window-dependent and flags the minority.
   More important, **miniprot's clean model already extends 5' by >= 30 bp in 1,386 of 2,322 rows where both models are clean (60%)** and 3' by >= 30 bp in 1,264 (`ct4.py`). Example: P. tritici-repentis MAT1-2-1 - exonerate local alignment begins at reference residue 109 of 343 (236 aa; next codon is a stop, so there is nothing to extend 3'), miniprot aligns from residue 9 and gives 335 aa, clean, own stop.
   "The problem is mostly 3'" is true for MAT1-1-1 in Pleosporales/Botryosphaeriales; it is not true for MAT1-2-1 or in general.
8. **Both tools are edge-limited, but for different reasons.** After extension, miniprot and exonerate end at **the same stop codon in 1,787 of 1,795** clean pairs (99.6%) - but in 1,590 of those the miniprot model is itself edge-ended and *my extension code* supplied its stop, so this "agreement" is by construction. The informative subset is the **205 rows where miniprot's own model ends in a stop codon** (it reached it by alignment, independent of any extension): **200 of 205 (97.6%) coincide with the exonerate extension's stop** (`conc.py`). That is real independent corroboration - but only 10% of extensions (clades: Ascochyta 25, Aspergillus 24, Didymella 20, Alternaria 18, Pyrenophora 15, Dothistroma 14, ...) and **zero in Macrophomina, Botryosphaeria, Aureobasidium, Hortaea, Venturia, Phyllosticta (1 in Cladosporium)**, the clades that dominate the extension count.

9. **Donor-site proxy for "the model ends at an exon, an intron follows" [V, `donor.py`, `dn.py`].** Fraction of clean extended models (3,000 rows) whose first two bases after the model end (gene orientation) are `GT`; chance is 6.25%, and the next codon of a coding frame is `GTN` (Val) ~6-7% of the time:
   overall 4-7% for every extension class (30-99: 6.9%, 100-199: 4.1%, 200+: 6.8%; complete models: 0.0% by definition), i.e. **no enrichment overall**. By clade it is not uniform: **Aureobasidium 24.2% (n=194), Friedmanniomyces 39.6% (48), Phyllosticta 12.7% (63)** versus Macrophomina 1.6%, Botryosphaeria 0%, Pyrenophora 0%, Venturia 0%, Hortaea 0%, Corynespora 0%.
   So in about a quarter of the Aureobasidium (and Friedmanniomyces) models the exonerate alignment ends exactly where a 5' splice site starts - a real exon end - and an extension would read intron as codons. Aureobasidium is the note's second lead example and the clade where ORF-only evidence is weakest (off-frames open >= 100 codons in 22%/32%), tail similarity beats nulls in 23% (margin >= 20 in 19%) and there is no independent stop (none of the 205 rows with a miniprot stop of its own is Aureobasidium). I would not extend Aureobasidium (or any clade with a donor-site excess) on ORF length alone; gate on "no `GT` donor at the model end unless independent support", and compute the donor proxy as a standard flag.
   (Absence of GT in Pyrenophora/Macrophomina/Botryosphaeria is itself informative: those models do not end at a donor, consistent with a protein alignment that simply ran out of similarity inside an exon.)

10. **Non-canonical splice sites and a motivating case the note misreads [V, `splice.py`, `gfftest`/`bf3.py` logs].** The P. tritici-repentis MAT1-1-1 intron (53-56 bp, in all 76 Pyrenophora MAT1-1-1 models) is **GC-AG** (exonerate GFF `splice_site "GC"` for Ptr134); 40.8% of all Pyrenophora introns in these models are GC-AG, and miniprot only places GT-AG introns (99.8% of its introns), which is why its Ptr134/Ptr902 model is one 1,259 bp exon with the intron coded as CDS and 6 "internal stops" (the "419 aa with 6 stops" the note calls "miniprot skipping an intron" is miniprot *coding* a GC-AG intron; in the other two Ptr genomes it reaches 345/396 aa clean with its own stop). The `GC-AG` rate (Fulvia 23.5%) and the 16% TA-donor introns in Aureobasidium exonerate models are splice-model problems the proposal has no signal for. The hard "internal stop => drop" filter is right to drop that miniprot model, and exonerate+extension (401 aa, GC-AG intron placed) is the better structure there; but the lesson is not "miniprot skips introns", it is "miniprot cannot represent GC-AG", which a splice-site check would detect directly and which makes miniprot's longer 5' ends in GC-AG clades (Pyrenophora, Fulvia) suspect.

## 4. Scoring and choice

**BLOSUM62 local score [V, `chooser_test.py`, `ct_all.tsv`, 3,494 gene rows, all MAT1-1-1/MAT1-2-1 canonical models, best reference of 26/29, Biopython PairwiseAligner local, open -11, extend -1 = `helixer_rule.query_score`].**
- A local alignment score cannot decrease when residues are appended: `score(exo+ext) >= score(exo)` always. Gain = 0 in 203 of 2,021 cases, <= 3 (inside `TIE_TOL`) in 236, <= 10 in 816, <= 20 in 1,249; median gain per tail residue 0.1 raw units; for tails >= 100 aa, 761 of 1,789 (43%) gain <= 10.
  By clade the median gain is 8 (Macrophomina, 489 models, median tail 200 aa), 11 (Aureobasidium), 8 (Botryosphaeria), 0 (Venturia: 83% gain <= 3) versus 48 (Pyrenophora), 112 (Didymella), 131 (Ascochyta).
  Hence **"scores within TIE_TOL are ties and the longer wins" reduces to "always take the extension" whenever an extension candidate exists**; the score plays no role in the extension decision, only in exonerate-vs-miniprot. The note says the tail alignment is "not used as a gate", but then presents a *scored* chooser; be honest that extension is unscored.
- `TIE_TOL = 3` is raw BLOSUM62 units (~1.5 bits): effectively an exact tie. The prototype used `round(score/3)` binning, which is not a tolerance (148.4 and 148.6 can land in adjacent bins); do not port it.
- **One reference per target** hides instability. Because exonerate/miniprot already align **all** curated references of the gene (`search.py:763-771`, `_select_requested_gene` `search.py:663-713` keeps the highest raw alignment score), the note's "against the one best curated reference" is a misdescription of the pipeline; the re-score picks another single reference by max SW over clean candidates. (Rule stability under changing the reference: see 4a.)
- **Effect on what gets chosen [V].** Rule A (the design: clean, highest score, ties <= 3 to longer, exonerate/miniprot x +/-ext): exo 613, exo+ext 758, mini 619, **mini+ext 1,157** of 3,147 rows with a clean candidate (347 rows have none). A miniprot-family model is chosen in **1,776 rows (56%)** (MAT1-1-1 899/1,714; MAT1-2-1 877/1,780).
  In 1,649 of those a clean exonerate-family candidate exists: miniprot wins by > 3 score in 1,171 (median +50 score, median +55 aa, 924 with > 30 aa longer), by <= 3 (decided by the length tie-break) in 478, of which 344 differ by <= 10 aa and 166 by <= 3 aa. 1,290 of the 1,649 differ at the 5' end by >= 30 bp.
  **So `choose_extend` as written is mainly a "make miniprot canonical and extend its 5' end" policy**, which contradicts 3.5 ("does not move the 5' end") and the staging argument; it changes the exon structure and coordinates of ~half of all canonical models, not only 3' ends.
- **Alternatives I tested on the same candidates** (`ct_all.tsv`, columns A-D): B = score minus 2 per unaligned residue (prototype-style length penalty): disagrees with A in 1,828 rows (58%) - choice is dominated by an arbitrary penalty; C = mean of the top-3 references' scores: disagrees with A in 80 rows (2.5%), i.e. consensus across references changes little;
  D = pyhmmer profile bit score (HMM built by MAFFT `--localpair` of the 26/29 curated references per gene, `hmmsearch`, best domain): disagrees with A in 226 rows (7%).
  Profile **envelope coverage** (hmm_to - hmm_from + 1 over model length M) of exo vs exo+ext: median 0.355 -> 0.603, gain >= 0.1 in 85% of extensions, but the bit-score gain is small (median +7.9 bits; <= 0 in 95 rows). Profile coverage is a more informative completeness signal than the length ratio and is cheap; it cannot discriminate real from spurious tails any better than SW (both are driven by the reference set).
- **Recommended simple deterministic design [R, built from the above]:** do not rank candidates by one scalar. (i) hard filters (cds_blocks-clean; 3'-closed after extension; total length <= 1.25 x longest curated; no overlap with a neighbouring modelled CDS); (ii) 3' extension is a *separate, unscored, flagged* step with an explicit evidence class (`orf_only`, `orf+tail_similarity`, `orf+independent_stop`); (iii) exonerate-vs-miniprot only when their structures differ by more than a tolerance at an end, decided by a **named ordered rule** (reference-aligned span first, profile bits second) and recorded as `model_ambiguity` rather than silently swapping canonical; (iv) record miniprot-own-stop agreement as `independent_support` (200/205 agree today): for Pyrenophora/P. teres miniprot (all references) is already clean and 3'-closed by its own stop in 4 of the 6 listed loci (the other two reach a stop but code the GC-AG intron), so the motivating clade does not *need* the extension to be right, whereas Aureobasidium (0/4 closed) is where it matters most and where it is least supported.
- **Test on the 35-genome prototype set** (`ct_test.tsv`, 62 rows incl. MAT1-2-1; compared with `test2_out.tsv` for the 38 MAT1-1-1 rows): my rule A agrees with the prototype's choice in 25/38 (C 24, D 23, B 17). Real differences: Aureobasidium (e.g. GCA_002156615: prototype mini 332 aa clean vs mine mini+ext 386 aa because the prototype's `ext>=20` and
  penalised-score logic skipped extending the miniprot model), Sclerotinia 3-way ties (prototype mini+ext 281 aa vs mine exo+ext 278 aa), Aureobasidium GCA_003992365 (prototype exo+ext 362 vs mini+ext 376 by profile). In P. tritici-repentis GCA_025541315 MAT1-1-1 my score picks miniprot 344 aa (score 701) over exo+ext 401 aa (666), while the two other Ptr genomes in the set pick exo+ext (401, 666 vs miniprot 418 aa **with a stop**, 436): the same locus structure gives two different calls across near-identical assemblies - the sign of a decision close to a discontinuity.
  Rows with no clean candidate (internal stops in every candidate: the 5 Botrytis/Aureobasidium cases) are left undecided by A, as designed.

(4a) **Stability under the reference choice [V, `ct_all2.tsv`, `ts.py`]:** re-running rule A with the 2nd, 3rd and 4th best single reference instead of the best changes the chosen candidate in 3.2%, 3.1% and 2.2% of the 3,147 rows (101 rows for the 2nd-best; every difference is an exonerate-vs-miniprot family swap, never an extension decision, which is unscored anyway). The "one reference per target" worry is therefore **minor**; the instability is in the tool choice (tie-breaks, 5' differences), not in the reference.

## 5. The HMM question

**Do I agree that extension + exonerate/miniprot suffices for the 3' problem and a gene finder is only for residuals?** Partly. For the 3' end of a clean, non-frameshift, fully-aligned model: yes. But the note's own survey shows the residual is not small and not mostly 3':
frameshift models (5.7% of models, 38% of "interrupted"), 5' truncation (79% no M; miniprot longer at 5' in 60% of pairs), and extension evidence-free for the dominant clades. A gene finder is the standard answer to **5'** (start codon + upstream intron) and to splice jumps, so I would not call it a late, residual option; I would call it the likely candidate generator for the 5' problem and decide after a 5' truth set.

Specific tools:
- **AUGUSTUS with protein-profile extension (PPX)** [R]: needs species parameters (a trained species model; from memory, shipped models exist for a handful of model fungi such as Botrytis, Fusarium, Neurospora, Aspergillus [X: I did not check the installed AUGUSTUS species list - AUGUSTUS is not installed in the env I used], and certainly not for Macrophomina/Aureobasidium/Pyrenophora - training needs annotations). PPX with a profile from the curated alignment constrains ORF structure and supplies start/stop/splice signals; it is the most credible "better gene model" for single loci, but it would need per-clade parameters and a run-time dependency; per locus it is cheap. Use as a *candidate generator* in the chooser, not canonical.
- **GeMoMa** [R]: projects annotated reference gene models (needs a reference genome + annotation per reference species and ideally RNA-seq). The curated database holds proteins/CDS coordinates, not reference genomes; it is a poor fit unless the ~170 curated genomes are fetched. Skip.
- **GeneWise with a profile** [R]: obsolete, unmaintained, behaves like exonerate protein2genome with a profile; not worth the dependency.
- **Helixer** [R/X]: genome-wide DL model, independently predicts start-to-stop CDS including UTR-ish features; its use for core genes is untested here (held-out test pending in the project notes). Cheapest independent "second opinion" for 3' ends and 5' starts where available; I did not run it [X].
- **exonerate `protein2genome:bestfit`** [V]: exists in the installed exonerate 2.4.0 (`exonerate --help` lists `protein2dna:bestfit protein2genome:bestfit`; the manual says: "a bestfit version of the protein2genome model, with which the entire protein is included in the alignment. It is currently only available when using exhaustive alignment"). It therefore **requires `-E yes`**, is incompatible with `--refine region` (which is what the pipeline uses) and with heuristic seeding, and is slow (see results below).

**What I ran on 21 loci [V] (4 *P. tritici-repentis* MAT1-1-1 + the Ptr134 MAT1-2-1, 2 *P. teres*, 4 *Aureobasidium* MAT1-1-1 + 1 MAT1-2-1, 9 complete-gene controls: Alternaria x2, Aspergillus x2, Parastagonospora x2, Zymoseptoria, Fusarium, Neurospora; the Neurospora control gave no alignment, 20 analysed) (`exo_exp3.py` -> `exo_exp3b.out.tsv`, `refine_test.py`, `bf3.py`; window +-4 kb unless stated; exonerate 2.4.0, miniprot as in `search.py`):**

1. **Several references.** The pipeline already aligns *all* curated references of the gene and keeps the highest raw score (`search.py:763-771`, `663-713`), so "give it 2-3 diverse references" is already the status quo (26 MAT1-1-1, 29 MAT1-2-1). Running exonerate against each reference **separately** (5-25 references per locus aligned): in all 10 extension loci (Ptr MAT1-1-1 x4, teres x2, Aureobasidium MAT1-1-1 x4) **not one single reference closes the 3' edge** (0 of 5-19 per locus); the longest single-reference model is 157-229 aa against 124-227 aa for the production choice (at most +25 aa); the extended totals are nearly reference-independent (Ptr 403-409 aa, Aureobasidium 360-386). In the 8 controls the production (all-reference) model is 3'-closed in 8/8 and single references close it in 1-5 of 5-25 (the others are shorter or fragmentary). So references do not fix the edge; the edge is about where *homology* ends.
   Miniprot behaves differently: with all references and the production selection (best GFF score) it reaches its own stop codon in Ptr 4/4 (345 and 396 aa clean; 419 aa with 6 internal stops in two genomes - the Ptr134/Ptr902 case, see item 4 below and 3.10), in *P. teres* 2/2 (373 aa clean) and in 8/8 controls; in Aureobasidium **0/4** (321, 332, 327, 240 aa; open 44-136 aa). Single-reference miniprot models of the same locus range 82-396 aa (e.g. Aspergillus MAT1-2-1: 107-339), i.e. miniprot is far more reference-sensitive than exonerate, and the pipeline's all-reference selection is doing real work.
2. **`--refine region` vs `--refine full` vs none.** Identical for Ptr, *teres* and all controls; in 3 of the 4 Aureobasidium MAT1-1-1 `--refine full` gives 192-224 aa instead of 124-149 aa (+68-75 aa at the 3' end; the 3' extension left over shrinks from 236-237 to 160-168; total aa+ext unchanged at 360-386). `--refine region` is therefore part of the edge problem in Aureobasidium. (`search.py` notes `--refine full` crashes deterministically on some windows; a crash fallback chain full -> region -> none would be cheap and is not in the note.)
3. **`protein2genome:bestfit`.** **Could not be completed - itself the finding.** What I ran (all `srun/sbatch -p short`, exonerate 2.4.0, `--model protein2genome:bestfit -E yes --maxintron 400`, one reference per run): (a) a 1.7 kb window around the P. tritici-repentis Ptr134 MAT1-1-1 locus against the 383 aa reference 5016_c5: killed by the 40 min job limit with no output (`bf2.py`, job 30229378); (b) six loci (Ptr MAT1-1-1 x2, P. teres, Aureobasidium x2, one complete Parastagonospora control), 1.6-1.8 kb windows, full 344-388 aa reference (`bf3.py`, array 30229603): none produced output in 1 h 30 min of wall time, then I cancelled them; (c) a reduced probe - only the C-terminal part of the reference (residues 161/191/201/221 to the end, 124-228 aa) against only the 650-900 bp window covering the unaligned extension region, for Ptr (25541395), P. teres (8086755), Aureobasidium (29473) and the Parastagonospora control (`bf4.py`, array 30232976): all four timed out at the 25 min cap inside exonerate, **including the control, which is a 124 aa query on a 650 bp window.** The same four probes with the production-style local model (`protein2genome`, heuristic, `--refine region`) took 0.1-0.3 s each.
So bestfit is >= 3-4 orders of magnitude slower (0.1-0.3 s vs > 1,500 s) than the production setting (seconds vs > 25 min for the smallest case; the exhaustive protein2genome DP evidently does not use `--maxintron` to bound the intron state and scales roughly with query x window^2), which makes it impractical as a per-locus run-time step on whole-gene windows (a gene window is 1.5-10 kb plus padding) without much smaller windows or a different tool. **I therefore have no result on whether bestfit closes the 3' edge or adds spurious alignment: do not cite bestfit as an option on the basis of this review**, and the note's "cheap lever" description is wrong on cost.
What I did learn [V]: (i) the manual semantics above (entire protein forced into the alignment; exhaustive only; incompatible with `--refine`); (ii) the *local* alignment of the C-terminal half of the reference against the extension region gives **no alignment at all** for Ptr, P. teres and Aureobasidium (default score threshold), but 100% identity over the full 124 aa for the Parastagonospora control: the extension tails of the three lead clades are not alignable to the nearest reference C-terminus by exonerate's own scoring, which is why bestfit - which forces an alignment - would, if it could be run, produce an alignment there by construction, and why any bestfit extension in those clades would be unconvincing without a null (not run). Not run [X]: bestfit on the 21 loci, miniprot/other global modes, any other tool.
4. **Splice signals (not asked, but decisive for the gene-finder question) [V, `splice.py`].** Introns of the 2,785 exonerate canonical MAT1-1-1/MAT1-2-1 models: GT-AG 93.4%, **GC-AG 3.3% (Pyrenophora 40.8%, 62 of 152 introns; Fulvia 23.5%)**, TA-AG 1.3% (**Aureobasidium 16%: 35 of 218 introns have a TA donor**, which does not occur in nature, so those introns are placed wrongly or the alignment is not splicing there). Miniprot's introns are 99.8% GT-AG. Consequence: miniprot cannot place the Pyrenophora MAT1-1-1 GC-AG intron (53-56 bp in all 76 Pyrenophora MAT1-1-1 models) and codes the intron as CDS (the 419 aa "6 internal stops" model; 1259 bp single exon = 588 bp exonerate CDS + 53 bp intron + 615 bp extension + 3 bp stop, I verified the arithmetic against the report exons), while exonerate's Aureobasidium introns are partly non-canonical. So splice-site scoring is a concrete, small, deterministic improvement (and the main thing a trained gene finder would add here), not a hypothetical residual.

## 7. Truth set and training plan

- **Is it sound?** The plan to build a truth set before thresholds is right. But "annotated proteins at the locus in BFD genomes" is **circular where it matters**: most annotations in this ecosystem are funannotate/Augustus/exonerate-evidence models that end at the same alignment edges; agreement between them and an extended model would be coin-toss either way, and disagreement is ambiguous. The note names the caveat but still makes it the primary truth.
- **What I would use, in order:**
  1. The **171 curated records already in `db/`** (26 MAT1-1-1, 29 MAT1-2-1, plus flanks): deposited CDS/protein from NCBI with exon structure, fully independent of the pipeline. The existing holdout machinery (`benchmark.py`, `docs/holdout-benchmark.md`: record/species/genus/family/order radii) already withholds a record and re-detects it; add one metric: **exact protein/exon structure and 3'/5' end offsets of the detected model vs the deposited CDS**. n is ~55 for the two main genes but it is the cleanest truth and gives the divergence gradient directly.
  2. RNA-seq-supported annotations only where the BFD tree has them (PASA-reannotated genomes; the BFD tree has `do_annotation*` and PASA directories [X: I did not open them]); label the support (full-length cDNA/PASA vs ab initio).
  3. UniProt/RefSeq MAT1-1-1/MAT1-2-1 proteins from species with manual or transcript-supported annotation (a few dozen across Pleosporales, Sordariomycetes, Eurotiales); use for length-class and C-terminal checks, not as exon truth.
  4. Cross-species conservation of the extended C-terminus (e.g. the Ptr/Pt `...NDAFRVGADEDATLPTFDGANNA`) as **consistency**, not truth.
  5. Splice-aligned RNA reads for the extension region only (spliced read crossing a donor right after the model end is direct evidence of the intron failure mode in 3.1).
- **Splits.** Leave-one-genus-out is the minimum but the data are heavily clonal (Macrophomina 490 rows, 2,756 genomes in 223 genera); weight per lineage cluster (the BFD `do_ANI` machinery), not per genome, and report per-clade results. Hold out whole clades for threshold *selection* (Macrophomina is a good one: biggest, no independent corroboration); keep the threshold for flagging fixed before opening the truth.
- **Learned chooser?** No. The decision has four candidates, <10 informative features and perhaps tens of independent labelled lineages. A logistic regression would overfit lineage identity (genus is perfectly collinear with GC, reference distance and extension class). Minimum viable evaluation: hand rule + curated-record holdout (n ~ 55) + ~100 lineage-balanced annotated/RNA-supported loci, with 5' and 3' errors reported separately, per clade, and "wrong promotion rate" as the primary harm metric.
- **Cheaper and more valuable (my ranking):** (i) fix the frameshift/cds_blocks handling and measure again; (ii) add the **extended, verified C-terminal-complete proteins from well-supported genera** (Pyrenophora, Alternaria, Ascochyta/Didymella where miniprot independently ends in a stop in 205 loci) to the reference roster as new clade references, which reduces reference divergence at the source and costs nothing at run time (but needs curator promotion; do not auto-promote, and do not use them for evaluating themselves); (iii) a profile-HMM completeness instrument (envelope coverage) built per order from the roster; (iv) splice PWMs only after (i)-(iii).
