What was found
  • v0.8.1 is released and verified. 23,214 of 23,570 genomes are identical to v0.8.0; all 356 differences trace to a listed change, none unexplained.
  • The slow genomes were a search-setting problem. tblastn -seg no costs minutes on a few reference proteins (SLA2, APN2, MAT1-1-1). Soft-masked SEG for those three gene groups only (MATPREDICT_TBLASTN_SEG_GENES) takes the two outlier genomes from 3,063 s and 1,685 s to 468 s and 288 s with identical loci, and leaves 296 of 300 Ascomycota and all 120 Basidiomycota genomes identical. It gives no speed-up on typical genomes (same-node ratio 1.05).
  • Old nodes explain most of the production slowdown. Chunks on the Opteron node c01 ran about 2.15 times slower than on the EPYC nodes. Job structure explains little.
  • Weak idiomorph calls are fragile. Where a call rests on one unpolished fragment under about 50 bits, small search changes flip it (#275). Reads agree with the baseline for XylFL0594 and PYCC9313; sequence models show two loci are real but their idiomorph is undetermined.

Pezizomycotina genomes take about 150 s each (138 s median lineage-routed, 37 s for the yeast fallback families, 27 s for Mucoromycotina), and they hold about 88% of the compute. --taxid is already passed for every genome, so routing is already phylum-scoped; the phylum-fallback genomes are the fast ones.

4. The tblastn -seg finding

Correction: same-node timing, the other panels and the per-query policies

The wall-time ratios of 0.47x and 0.48x reported earlier compared new runs on EPYC nodes with baseline runs that were mostly on the older c01 nodes, so they did not measure SEG. On one node (60 Ascomycota genomes, 16 at a time on r12) SEG off took a median of 82 s (total 4,564 s) and soft-masked SEG 86 s (4,815 s): no speed-up on typical genomes (1.05x). The SEG-off run reproduces the v0.8.1 baseline exactly (60 of 60 genomes). The benefit is confined to outlier genomes. On Basidiomycota, soft-masked SEG on every query loses the short pheromone precursors (MFalpha, pheromone_B43, fungal_mating_type_pheromone), the reason -seg no was chosen, so it is applied per query:

With the gene list, the four remaining Ascomycota differences are span changes: two of 9 and 27 bp, and two of 12 to 20 kb at a locus edge (a flank-gene hit that shortens under filtering). The fraction of a query that is low-complexity does not predict its time (slow queries 6–17% masked, fast ones up to 40%), so a rule by gene name, measured per gene, is used instead.

Status: a v0.9.0 candidate, not part of v0.8.1: MATPREDICT_TBLASTN_SEG=yes with MATPREDICT_TBLASTN_SEG_GENES=SLA2,APN2,MAT1-1-1. It protects outlier genomes without changing any call on 510 test genomes. Still open: other lineages could have their own slow proteins, so the gene list needs a timing check per family. Experimental branch seg-switch (local, not pushed).

The flips come from two fragile steps, not from the raw hits (for Microdochium the best MAT1-2-1 hit is 59.3 bits with SEG off and 58.9 on). The overlap resolver compares the identity of single short HMG-box hits (first_pass_identity), and the bitscore vote compares bitscores across different genes. The same weakness produced the T. viride error in the pilot. Both Microdochium and Yamadazyma are flank-carried loci whose idiomorph was never well supported.

The miniprot checks use the same assembly and reference proteins as detection: an independent model, not independent evidence. Only the XylFL0594 and PYCC9313 checks use reads (first reads of mate 1 for PYCC9313, so a head-of-run sample).

7. Read data handoff

A handoff of how MATPredict identified and fetched fungal read data, and the replies to the PopSeq agent, are in /bigdata/stajichlab/shared/projects/Fungi/PopSeq/specifications/ (MATpredict_spec_handoff.md, MATpredict_reply_to_004.md, MATpredict_feedback_on_002_005.md). Measured from 4,548 run records: AWS Open Data median 10.3 MB/s per task (p10 3.2, p90 20.1) over 4,211 runs and 6,343 GB; unavailable_at_ncbi in 25 runs (0.55%), and the NCBI fallback rescued none of 26. Reads are never cached by MATPredict: the .sra and the subsample go to $SCRATCH and are deleted on exit.
